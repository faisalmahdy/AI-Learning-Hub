# Logo brief — AI-Learning-Hub

> Produced 2026-08-19 by a three-stage consultation workflow: senior-designer draft brief → veteran brand-consultant critique → prompt-smith → adversarial logo-craft critic. All five original draft directions were revised or replaced.

## Brand essence

**Learning that must prove itself.**

## Consultant critique of the draft strategy

What the brief gets right is rare: the design system already exists in production and is coherent, and the brief correctly identifies that signal-orange is not decoration but a semantic — it means "earned," and protecting a single accent with a fixed meaning is worth more than any logo. It is also honest about audience in a way edtech briefs almost never are (the builder first, learners later), which frees the mark from having to look welcoming. Where it fails is that it lists six brand truths and never names an essence, so the designer had nothing to reduce to and defaulted to illustrating features: four bars for levels, nine dots for nine topics, three rungs for three levels. That is a legend for the dashboard, not a mark — and it is data-bound in a brand whose own repo records the taxonomy moving from eight tracks to nine topics one week ago, so any literal count is a promise the curriculum will break. The brief also buries its single most differentiating fact: `ledger/recall-ledger.json` is empty, zero dated passes, and the site says so out loud; that admission is the brand, filed here under "personality" and exploited by none of the five directions, when the correct instruction is that the mark must look right mostly unlit. The doctrine's sharpest clause — "every number gets an uncertainty" — has no visual expression anywhere in the set, and D1 is its exact opposite: bars without error bars are a claim of certainty, which this brand would fail in review. D1 also asks to "evolve the placeholder," which is sunk-cost reasoning; a placeholder's only job is to be replaced. The usage list understates the real constraint: the mark must hold as charcoal-on-white AND as orange on a near-black rail at 26px, and "works in one color" on one ground is not the test that kills thin linework. Finally, since the mark carries no letters, the wordmark does all the naming work and the brief never specs the lock-up or the IBM Plex Mono relationship — and it never says what the mark must not do, which is why D5 arrived as a literal H in a no-letterforms brief.

## The five directions

### Option 1 — The Interval (`interval-dot`)

**Concept.** REPLACES D1. A horizontal charcoal hairline span at roughly 5:1, closed at each end by a short vertical cap of identical weight, the caps about 40% of the mark's height; terminals square-cut, no taper anywhere. A solid signal-orange dot sits on the span off-center — about 38% in from the left cap, never the midpoint. Three strokes and a dot, nothing else; in one color the dot goes solid charcoal and the span stays hairline so the dot still reads as mass.

**Symbolism.** The doctrine drawn literally: a measured value with its uncertainty around it. The dot sits off-center because a real confidence interval is asymmetric — the mark refuses to idealize itself, which is the same reflex that makes this site admit an empty ledger. Orange on the dot means the reading was earned.

**Why strong.** Reduces to one idea and one sentence, and no edtech or AI brand is using an error bar — the whole category draws growth, never precision. Three strokes survive 16px, survive a 26px rail in orange-on-charcoal, and emboss or foil-stamp cleanly on a certificate because the geometry is all straight lines and one disc. It is the only direction that makes the brand's governing doctrine visible instead of illustrating the dashboard.

**Risk.** Rotated vertical it becomes a serif I, so orientation must be locked and the caps kept short — a real hazard under a no-letterforms constraint. It is also the most abstract mark in the set: it needs the wordmark to teach it once, and at 16px the dot has to be at least a quarter of the mark's height or the whole thing collapses into a plain dash.

### Option 2 — Nine Cells (`nine-cells`)

**Concept.** REVISES D2 — keep the count and the single lit unit, delete every connecting line. A 3x3 grid of squares in thin charcoal stroke, gutters about one third of a cell, corner radius about one eighth of a cell. Exactly one cell — second column, bottom row, deliberately off both diagonals — is solid signal orange with no stroke. Grid is optically square overall; no frame, no labels, no lines between cells.

**Symbolism.** The curriculum as an inventory rather than a ladder: nine topics held, exactly one thing actually earned. The eight empty cells are the honest half of the mark, matching a ledger that today holds zero passes, and the same glyph can be re-struck with more cells lit for milestone certificates without a redesign.

**Why strong.** It is the site's own grammar — heat cells and ladder segments — compressed into a glyph, so the mark and the product look manufactured by the same hand. Nine squares is the natural resolution of a 16px favicon (three 4px blocks, 2px gutters), which is why it will be the sharpest of the five at avatar size. One-color version is trivially eight outlines and one solid, and it uses orange exactly as the product already taught the viewer to read it.

**Risk.** 3x3 grids are a crowded convention — app launchers, tile and window marks — so it survives only on the asymmetric lit cell and strict stroke discipline; drop either and it becomes generic instantly. It also says collection, not difficulty: the three-level spine goes entirely unsaid, and it shares a rectilinear cell language with the Off-Register Strike direction, so those two should not be shortlisted together.

### Option 3 — Re-lit (`decay-relit`)

**Concept.** REPLACES D3 — mountains and planted flags are achievement-badge stock, one shelf over from the mortarboard. Instead: a single shallow charcoal arc falling left to right across about two thirds of the width, drawn like a slack cable at constant thin weight with square-cut terminals and no swoosh taper. One short vertical signal-orange stroke cuts it at roughly 70% of the width and rises to stop just above the arc's starting height. Two elements only, in a 3:2 field.

**Symbolism.** Forgetting is the default state and dated recall is the intervention — 'assets are not learning' rendered as a mechanism instead of a slogan. The orange vertical is the closed-book pass that lifts the curve back above where it began, which is precisely what a boss fight logged in the ledger does.

**Why strong.** The only direction about time, which is this brand's actual gate — every module is closed by a dated pass, not by reading. It is contrarian in a category where every logo depicts ascent, so it cannot be mistaken for a competitor, and two strokes hold up in one color and at small sizes. It reads as an instrument trace, which sits naturally beside IBM Plex Mono and thin linework.

**Risk.** An arc plus a rising tick can read as a stock-chart recovery, and if the arc acquires any weight variation it drifts toward a swoosh — the most dangerous silhouette it could accidentally borrow. At 16px the fall tends to flatten into a dash, so the vertical must carry the entire idea at favicon size.

### Option 4 — The Dent (`dented-radar`)

**Concept.** REVISES D4 — keep the instrument lineage, throw away the sweep and the caught dot, which is what made it read military. An irregular six-vertex polygon in thin charcoal, plotted on an implied but undrawn hexagonal axis set: two vertices out near the full radius, one vertex collapsed to roughly 10% radius, forming a deep dent toward the center. A small solid signal-orange dot marks the dented vertex. No axis lines, no rings, no fill, no circle.

**Symbolism.** This is the site's own skills radar told truthfully — agents at 0.94, internals at 0.06 — so the brand's self-portrait ships with its hole visible. The orange dot marks the dent not as a failure but as the next thing to be earned, which is exactly how the dashboard frames its weakest cluster.

**Why strong.** A deliberately lopsided polygon is a shape virtually no brand will adopt, which makes it the most ownable and the most 'belongs to THIS brand' mark in the set. It silhouettes as one distinctive odd shape at favicon size instead of dissolving into detail, and it works in one color as an outline with a filled vertex. It converts honesty from a line in the brief into geometry, which is the only place a personality claim can survive.

**Risk.** Asymmetry reads as a mistake to viewers who lack the radar context, and at small sizes it drifts toward chipped-gem and crystal marks. It also freezes a data snapshot: once the weak clusters improve, the mark asserts something no longer true — a brand governed by 'measure or it didn't happen' may eventually find its own logo out of date.

### Option 5 — Off-Register Strike (`struck-cell`)

**Concept.** REPLACES D5 outright — two rails with three rungs is a capital H, which the hard constraints forbid; this is not a risk to mitigate, it is a disqualification. Instead: one thin charcoal square outline (a ledger cell) with a solid signal-orange square about 72% its size struck inside it, rotated 10 to 12 degrees and deliberately overshooting one edge by roughly 8% so it breaks the outline. Two squares, one machined, one hand-landed; nothing else.

**Symbolism.** A pass is struck into the record, not awarded — closed-book, dated, irreversible. The precise outline is the system and the off-register overshoot is the human who took the boss fight, which is the lab-notebook truth of a hub built and used by one engineer.

**Why strong.** The only direction that reads as a physical impression, which is exactly what certificates, wax-seal-style stamps and stickers need; the other four are traces and would look like report headers on a certificate. It carries the most weight of the five in a single color because it works in mass rather than line, and two contrasting shapes are unmistakable at 16px where fine linework fails.

**Risk.** Rotated-square-inside-square is a recognized abstract logo device, so its entire claim to originality rests on the overshoot — if a production tidy-up straightens it, the mark is anonymous. At 26px on the rail a 10-degree tilt can read as a rendering misalignment rather than intent, and it shares a rectilinear vocabulary with Nine Cells.
